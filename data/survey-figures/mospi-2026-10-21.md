# Government survey data for Mārgadarshak — Odisha and All-India

Pulled 21 October 2026 through the MoSPI data API (Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation). Each figure names its survey. These are **population averages for context and product decisions**. They are not rules, not rule cards, and must never be shown to a student as a prediction about them.

## 1. Private coaching (NSS 80th round, Comprehensive Modular Survey: Education, 2025)

Share of school students taking or having taken private coaching in the current academic year:

| Level | Odisha | All-India |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | 59.7% | 22.9% |
| Middle | 63.2% | 29.6% |
| Secondary (9–10) | **68.7%** | 37.8% |
| Higher Secondary (11–12) | 49.1% | 37.0% |
| All levels | **57.1%** | 27.0% |

Average yearly coaching spend per student who reported coaching (₹):

| Level | Odisha rural | Odisha urban | Odisha all | All-India all |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Secondary | 8,652 | 15,137 | 9,908 | 11,096 |
| Higher Secondary | 12,175 | **30,236** | 21,188 | 17,323 |

Who pays (first major source of funding): household members, 89.2% in Odisha (95.0% All-India); government scholarship, **2.3%** in Odisha (1.2% All-India).

**What this means for the app:** coaching is the norm in Odisha, not the exception. That fits the founder case study, where a tuition teacher chose his college. A parent warning about paid referrals (Phase 4, Parent Mode) is well grounded. Scholarships are the main funding for very few students, so finding scholarships is a real gap.

## 2. School dropout and transition (UDISE+ 2024-25)

| Odisha | Value |
|---|---|
| Dropout, Primary (1–5) | 0.7% |
| Dropout, Upper Primary (6–8) | 3.2% |
| Dropout, Secondary (9–10) | **15.0%** |
| Transition, Class 10 → Class 11 | **71.9%** |

For comparison, Secondary dropout in 2024-25 was 20.0% in West Bengal, 15.5% in Andhra Pradesh, 11.5% in Maharashtra and 4.8% in Kerala. The API returned no All-India row.

**What this means for the app:** about 28 in 100 Odisha students do not move from Class 10 into Class 11 in the next year. Some take ITI, a diploma or other routes that UDISE does not count as Class 11. This is Moment A, and it supports putting Class 10 and the ITI/Diploma tracks first (APP_PLAN Phase 3).

## 3. Unemployment by qualification (PLFS, calendar year 2025, usual status PS+SS, all ages)

| Highest qualification | Odisha | All-India |
|---|---|---|
| Secondary | 3.1% | 2.1% |
| Higher Secondary | 5.4% | 4.7% |
| Diploma / certificate | 10.6% | 6.9% |
| Graduate | **16.0%** | 11.2% |
| Post-graduate & above | 11.0% | 10.0% |

Youth aged 15–29, all qualifications: Odisha 17.2% (2022), 11.8% (2023), 11.6% (2024), **10.6% (2025)**. All-India: 9.9% (2025).

**What this means for the app:** in Odisha, unemployment rises with qualification up to a degree. This supports Moments E and F: a degree alone is not a plan, and students need a backup route. Do not turn this into "a degree is not worth it". The rate counts only people seeking work, and it says nothing about pay or job quality.

## Limits

- Survey averages describe groups, not any one student. They are never used for eligibility, ranking or advice to an individual.
- Each section has its own year: coaching 2025, UDISE 2024-25, PLFS 2025, NEET youth 2022-23, course spend 2017-18. Always show the year.
- AISHE higher-education data in the API stops at 2021-22, so it was not used.
- The PLFS rates by qualification cover all ages. The API did not return a state breakdown by both age and qualification.
- Where these figures go into public copy (website, evidence library), recheck them against the published MoSPI/UDISE report tables first.
