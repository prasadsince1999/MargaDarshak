## Platform Strategy

### LinkedIn (Primary — start here)

Best for: founder story, product updates, student voice case analysis, system-level education posts, launch updates, trust-building with parents/educators/professionals.

Tone: polished, thoughtful, factual, emotionally controlled.

### Instagram

Best for: quote cards, student stories, short reels, carousel explainers, app screenshots, "one wrong decision / one better path" visuals.

Tone: modern, visual, crisp, emotionally clear.

### X / Twitter

Best for: short insights, build-in-public notes, product thoughts, quick feature drops, sharp observations on student decision problems.

Tone: direct, sharp, concise.

### Reddit & Quora

Best for: genuine answers to student problems, not promotional posts.

**Strategy:**
- Find posts like "PCM or commerce?", "I missed exam forms," "Can I switch after PCB?", "My parents want safe options."
- Answer with structure: stage, options, constraints, risks, backup routes.
- Mention Margadarshak only lightly, e.g. "I'm building a system around this exact problem."
- Never spam links in every answer.

### YouTube Shorts

Best for: UI demos, "after 10th what next," "backup routes matter."

### WhatsApp

Best for: status clips, posters, launch countdown, referral asks, one-line trust identity.

## Post Framing Rules

### App Updates (Don't be boring)

**Bad:** "Added document readiness module."

**Better:** "A student can lose admission over a name mismatch, missing category proof, or wrong certificate format. Margadarshak now checks document readiness earlier, before that panic starts."

Rule: Always say what changed **for the student**, not just "feature added."

### Student Stories (Ethics matter)

Best structure:
- The situation
- What went wrong
- Why it matters
- What a better system should have shown earlier

**Good example:**
> Student Voice 01
> A student filled only one major exam form, assuming it would work out. When results went badly, every backup deadline had already passed. This is not just exam stress. This is decision invisibility. Margadarshak is being built so one preparation path shows backup routes before time runs out.

**Bad:** "Look how sad this student is" / "So many students are suffering" / "This is heartbreaking"

Rule: Student stories should end with visibility/design/better systems, not sadness.

### Parent Posts

Rule: Lead with data, not emotion. Parents care about fewer wrong decisions, fewer wasted years, less hidden risk, more visible backup routes, fewer costly mistakes, more informed family decisions.

### General

Rule: Never make the page sound like a sad-problem page. Always move toward clarity, structure, and progress.

## UGC Video Letters

Format: calm, honest, 20-40 seconds, direct to camera.

Templates:
- "Dear student in Class 10…"
- "Dear parent deciding after 12th…"
- "Dear dropper who thinks one exam is everything…"
- "Dear student who chose the wrong stream…"

Each ends with one clear insight like "backup routes should be visible before results day" or "one wrong form can cost a year."
