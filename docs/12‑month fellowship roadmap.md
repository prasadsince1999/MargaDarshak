## 1. OSV application narrative (draft)

You can adapt this for “What are you working on?” + “Why you?” + “What will you do with the fellowship?” style questions.

### a) One‑sentence description

> I’m building Margadarshak, a parallel guidance system for Indian students and parents that combines verified roadmaps, skill tests, and AI explanation to replace guesswork and coaching‑center gossip with clear, personalized paths from Class 9 to first job.[^1]

### b) Expanded project description (story style)

> In India, a 16‑year‑old will spend more time researching which phone to buy than which degree will shape the next 40 years of their life. Most students choose streams and courses based on relatives’ opinions, random YouTube videos, or the loudest coaching centre, not on a clear understanding of their own skills, interests, and the real options available to them. Parents are deeply involved in these decisions but usually have even less structured information than their children.[^1]
>
> I want to build a new layer of infrastructure around this problem: Margadarshak. It is not a “career app” or another test‑prep platform. It is a **student + parent guidance system** that does three things:
> - builds a verified knowledge graph of Indian education and career pathways (streams, degrees, exams, government schemes, private tracks),
> - measures a student’s strengths and preferences through practical, game‑like assessments they can complete in front of their parents or teachers,
> - and turns that into clear, honest roadmaps explained in simple language through an AI mentor inside a mobile app.[^1]
>
> In student mode, a teenager sees: “Here is where you are. These are the realistic paths from here. This is what each path demands in subjects, effort, marks, exams, time, and money. Here are backup options if the first plan fails.” In parent mode, the same system shows: “Here is what your child is actually good at, here are the fields they can thrive in, here is what those paths cost and how long they take, and here is how to support them without breaking them.” A shared “family bridge” view lets both sit together, compare paths visually, and discuss options based on real data instead of pressure.[^1]
>
> The second layer is **diagnostics and pedagogy**. I don’t want students to just read about careers; I want to measure what they are naturally good at. I will partner with local teachers and counselors to run low‑stress, game‑like tests—communication tasks, spatial puzzles, basic logic, domain tasters—either online with live camera supervision or in community centres, then feed those results into the app so each child’s roadmap reflects their actual skills and interests, not just exam scores. My inspiration here is how a great DSA teacher explains patterns: first show the real‑world applications, then imitate how a human would think on paper, then encode that into algorithms and variations. You can do the same with any subject or career: application → mental model → pattern library → practice.[^1]
>
> Under the hood, Margadarshak uses structured data for all official facts—eligibility rules, subjects, exam names, schemes, percentages, job outcomes—and uses AI only for explanation, personalization, and translation into parent‑friendly language. That makes it safer and more trustworthy than a raw chatbot. Technically, I’m building this with Flutter, Isar for local‑first storage, Firebase/Auth/Firestore/Cloud Functions for the backend, and Vertex AI / Gemini for the AI layer, reusing patterns I already built in my previous project Jarvis.[^1]

### c) Why you?

> I am a self‑taught developer from Bhubaneswar, Odisha. I did not have access to elite counselors or famous colleges; I learned Flutter, Dart, AI tools, English speaking, and mental math mostly from the internet and my own experiments. Because I didn’t have clear guidance, I made the same mistakes I now see everywhere: choosing degrees without understanding the path, discovering my real interests late, and losing years to confusion.[^1]

It has 60+ tools, hundreds of tests, and a full README that describes how to translate a 2,500‑year‑old philosophy into working code. Jarvis is my proof‑of‑work that I can design systems, not just screens. It also taught me that I don’t want to build “another productivity app” for OSV—I want to use the same skills to attack one concrete, painful problem: my country’s guidance gap.[^2][^3]
>
> Margadarshak is the focused, real‑world version of everything I’ve been learning: structured data + local‑first design + AI flows, used not for shiny demos but to prevent millions of students from making blind educational choices. I am not coming to OSV with a slide deck; by the time of selection I aim to have a working app, documented architecture, initial roadmaps, and pilot families using it.[^1]

### Months 9–10: Scale content + refine interaction

    - high‑competition paths (IIT, NEET, UPSC foundation)

- Expand pedagogy:
    - more “crunch” modules where you show only the essential pattern view of a subject, with visuals and mini‑games.

    - in‑app booking for a 30–60 minute call where counselor + parent + student look at roadmaps together.


### Months 11–12: Sustainability, open knowledge, and next phase

- Analyze pilot outcomes:
    - how many students changed their plan?
    - where diagnostics and roads agreed/disagreed with parent expectations?
- Design a **financial model** (see next section) that keeps the core product extremely affordable while supporting a small stable team.
- Open‑source a subset of the **“guidance graph”** (e.g., JSON schemas or APIs for Indian education paths) so others can build on top. This fits OSV’s love for open infrastructure projects.
- Prepare a public launch:
    - website
    - explainer video
    - stories from your pilots
    - clear invitation for other schools to join.

## 3. How to earn money without breaking your mission

Your confusion is valid: you want maximum impact, but you also need to eat and pay a team. Let’s design a **hybrid model** that OSV will see as sustainable, not extractive.

### Principles

- **Core guidance should feel like a public good**, especially for low‑income families.
- **You charge for high‑touch human time**, not for simply showing exam names.
- **Schools / institutions help cross‑subsidize individuals** (B2B helps keep B2C cheap).


### Layered model

Think in three layers:

#### 1) Always‑free or nearly‑free layer (for everyone)

- App download, onboarding, and basic roadmaps.
- Eligibility summaries, backup paths, basic diagnostics.
- Access to “crunched” study material (short, high‑quality concept explanations).
- AI mentor answers in a limited daily quota.

This can be funded by:

- OSV money in year one
- Later: institutional contracts and a small amount of higher‑tier revenue.


#### 2) Low‑cost paid services (optional, for families who can afford)

This is where you earn honestly:

- **One‑to‑one or family counseling sessions**
    - Pricing: cheap by global standards, but meaningful in INR (e.g., ₹500–₹1500 per 45–60 minute session depending on region).
    - These are high‑value: your counselor interprets diagnostics + roadmaps + context and helps the family choose.
- **Deeper diagnostic reports**
    - Free tier: simple strengths summary.
    - Paid tier: detailed multi‑page report with recommendations and follow‑up plan.
- **Structured programs**
    - 4‑week “Find your path after 10th” online cohort with live sessions + app usage.
    - 8‑week DSA / foundational subject bootcamps that follow your pattern‑based teaching style.
