**PW School of Startups: a ₹100 crore fund committed to backing 100 startups over 60 months** — mentorship, training, and capital.

That is a **named programme with an application process**, not a cold DM to a billionaire. It is your entry point. Apply there first; a warm introduction from inside PW is worth ten unanswered messages.

**Their stated acquisition thesis:** content-heavy categories · geographic expansion, especially South India · anything giving access to **new content and users**.

Mārgadarshak sits squarely inside "content-heavy" — 61 roadmaps, a verified exam database, and now state-level path data.

## PART 2 — The tension. Read this before you write anything.

**Physics Wallah is a coaching company. Mārgadarshak's positioning refuses coaching-industry practices.**

This is not a small thing, and going in without having thought it through would be a mistake.

- Your own product copy names *"coaching ads"* as part of the problem students face.
- Last week's audit found that **"JEE Foundation" and "NEET Foundation"** — coaching marketing constructs — had to be purged from your app as false exams.
- Your first law is: *no student's future can be sold to the highest-paying institution.*
- Mārgadarshak will sometimes tell a student **"you don't need coaching for this route"** — which is revenue-negative for any coaching business.

**But the alignment is real, and it is deeper than the tension.**

Alakh's entire origin is *anti*-expensive-coaching. He served exactly the students you serve — tier-2 and tier-3, price-sensitive, first in their family to attempt this. The rural learning centre was not a marketing exercise. He has already demonstrated the instinct your product is built on.

### The insight that makes this a pitch rather than a request

> **Mārgadarshak is upstream of Physics Wallah.**

PW's funnel begins when a student *already knows* they want JEE or NEET. Mārgadarshak serves the moment **before** that — the Class 10 student who does not yet know whether engineering is even the right direction, or the diploma student who has never heard of lateral entry.

That moment is invisible to PW today. It is also the moment where most of the damage is done: a student who picks the wrong stream at 16 arrives at coaching already misdirected, and no amount of teaching quality fixes that.

**You are not competing for his students. You are the layer that would send him better-informed ones — and honestly tell the rest that his product is not what they need.** That honesty is precisely what would make the association valuable to him rather than embarrassing.

## PART 3 — The ask: four things, and nothing else

PrasaD's position, stated plainly. **This clarity is the strongest card in the pitch** — most founders ask for money and quietly hand over control. Asking for exactly four things and refusing the rest signals someone who has thought past the cheque.

### What you want

| | What | Why PW is uniquely able to give it |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Financial support** | The ₹100 Cr School of Startups fund exists for precisely this |
| **2** | **Talented people** who believe in the idea | PW has engineers, designers, content and curriculum people. You are one person |
| **3** | **Their students as organic early users** — real feedback | Millions of students. Giving you 500 beta testers costs them almost nothing and is worth more than any amount of money |
| **4** | **Network and community** | Introductions to educators, school counsellors, state-level contacts — the people who can verify path data you cannot check from Bhubaneswar |

### What you are not asking for, and will not accept

**No product control. No integration. No equity in exchange for editorial influence.**

- **No preferential surfacing of PW courses.** Ever. Payment never changes fit score, trust score or student-voice score. This is the first law and it is not tradeable.
- **Mārgadarshak stays free for students.** Permanently.
- **The paths repo stays open** — public, forkable, correctable by anyone.
- **The app keeps the right to say "you don't need coaching for this route"** when that is true.
- **You keep the brand and the source.** If it becomes "PW Mārgadarshak", the no-pay-to-rank promise stops being believable to students — even if you honour it perfectly.

### Say this out loud in the conversation

> *"I'm not asking you to own it. I'm asking you to back it. It has to stay independent and free — that's the only reason a student will trust what it tells them, and it's the only reason it's worth funding."*

That sentence does more work than a deck. It tells him the product's integrity is not negotiable, which is exactly what makes it valuable to be associated with.

### And say the second thing too

> *"I won't stop at one app. This is the first of several."*

**Investors back founders, not products.** School of Startups exists to find people who will keep building. Saying it plainly turns a grant request into a long-term bet — and it happens to be true.

**Preferred structures:** CSR/philanthropic grant · School of Startups portfolio place · distribution partnership.
❌ **Acquisition** — would end the product's credibility whatever the intentions. Not on the table.

## PART 4 — The message

**Send it to PW School of Startups first.** Use X or LinkedIn to Alakh only as a secondary route — keep it under 150 words there.

### Short version — X / LinkedIn DM

> Alakh sir — I'm building **Mārgadarshak**, a free career decision system for Indian students from Class 9 to PG. Not coaching. It answers the questions that come *before* coaching: which stream, which exams am I eligible for, what if I chose wrong, what's my backup.
>
> While building it I found that apps in this space list exams that no longer exist — NTSE has been stalled since 2021 and never accepted Class 9 students; "JEE Foundation" isn't a real exam at all. So I built a verified database and made the path data **public and open source**, so teachers in every state can correct what I can't check alone from Bhubaneswar.
>
> It's free, it stays free, and it takes no money from institutions for rankings.
>
> I'm not looking to be acquired. I'm looking for four things — funding, a few talented people who believe in it, some of your students as early users giving honest feedback, and your network to help verify state-level data. It stays independent.
>
> I'd like to apply to PW School of Startups. Would you look?
>
> github.com/prasadsince1999 · ksmxtech.com

### The reasons this works

**It leads with a finding, not a request.** *"NTSE has been stalled since 2021"* is a fact he can verify in thirty seconds, and it proves you did work nobody else did.

**It names what you refuse.** *"Takes no money from institutions for rankings"* signals you are not a lead-gen product — the single most important thing to establish with someone who has been pitched a thousand times.

**It uses his own door.** Asking about School of Startups is a real, answerable request. "Please fund me" is not.

**It does not flatter.** He receives that all day. A verified fact and a clear ask are rarer.

## PART 5 — The presentation

Keep it to **eight slides.** He is a teacher — he will respect structure and dislike padding.

| # | Slide | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The moment** | A Class 10 student choosing a stream with half the information. One real student's story — not a market-size chart. |
| 2 | **What's broken** | Scattered information · coaching ads before facts · parents seeing cost too late · no backup route. Your own line: *"Most students do not fail because they are weak. They lose years because they choose with half information."* |
| 3 | **What I found** | NTSE stalled since 2021 and never open to Class 9. RIMC is Class VIII only. "JEE Foundation" is a marketing construct. **Apps are telling students to prepare for exams that do not exist.** |
| 4 | **What I built** | 61 roadmaps · 11 stages · 17 features · verified exam database · Class 9 to PG. Screenshots, not adjectives. |
| 5 | **The one rule** | *No student's future can be sold to the highest-paying institution.* Say why: it costs revenue and that is the point. |
| 6 | **Open by design** | The paths repo is public. Teachers in every state can correct what one person in Bhubaneswar cannot verify alone. **Show the repo.** |
| 7 | **Where I'm stuck** | Free forever means no revenue. Data verification across 28 states needs people. State rules change annually. Be specific about what money would buy. |
| 8 | **The ask** | The four things: funding · a few talented people · students as early users · network for state-data verification. Then the line: *"I'm not asking you to own it. I'm asking you to back it."* Close with: *"This is the first app, not the only one."* |

### What to leave out
No hockey-stick projections. No "we are the Uber of X". No claiming users you do not have. **You are pitching honesty — the deck must model it.** Say plainly that the app is not yet on Play Store and why.

### What to prepare for
He will ask **"how does this make money?"** Do not dodge it. The true answer is strong: *Book Is Your Friend earns so Mārgadarshak stays free. I built the paid product specifically so the free one would never need to compromise.* That answer will do more for you than any revenue model.
